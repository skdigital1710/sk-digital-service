Part 2 · Questions 32–37

English responses · Copy each answer directly, then review before submission.

Question 32

In our view, matters like Nikah, Talaq, Khula, Mehr, Nafaqa, Wasiyyat and Meeraas are religious and personal matters and should not be made uniform through the UCC. These are not just social customs for Muslims, but are connected with their religious injunctions. Article 25 of the Constitution provides for freedom of religion. Therefore, it is not necessary to abolish Islamic law in the name of justice and legal facility.

Question 33

Difficulties include lack of knowledge about procedures, legal ignorance, delays in courts and disputes in Meeraas. The solution is not UCC, but easier legal process, free legal aid, proper registration of Nikah and protection of heirs' rights. Reforms are needed, but without replacing religious personal laws.

Question 34

We do not support any injustice, oppression or inequality against women. Mehr, Nafaqa, Khula and Meeraas etc. are legitimate rights of women and must be protected. But without understanding the complete context of a religious law, it is not just to label it as unjust. Where there is practical discrimination, legal reform and effective implementation should be ensured, not the complete uniformity of all religious laws.

Question 35

The rules of inheritance should remain different for different religious communities and the freedom of religion should be respected. For Muslims, the rules are based on Islamic teachings. A uniform system for all religions is not suitable. However, every heir must get their rightful share, illegal possession should be stopped, and the distribution should be made transparent — this is the responsibility of the government.

Question 36

Every child should have their dignity, upbringing, education and basic needs protected. However, the rules of maintenance and inheritance are not the same and depend on lineage and relevant legal provisions. The rights of children must be protected, but a child born without marriage has no share in inheritance. Whatever is given out of one's own will is a personal matter. For this, it is not necessary to end Islamic law.

Question 37

Where there is difficulty in legal process, injustice or delay in justice, reforms can be made as required. But before any reform, consultation with ulama, legal experts and concerned people is essential, and the religious and social implications must be studied. Our stance is to ensure justice, women's rights and children's protection, but without affecting religious freedom and Islamic law.
